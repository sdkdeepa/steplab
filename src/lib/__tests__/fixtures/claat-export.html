In this codelab you'll build a real web app by describing it in plain English. You won't write any code.

What you'll learn

  • How to turn an idea into a working app with prompts
  • How to improve an app one small prompt at a time
  • How to test AI output instead of trusting it
  • What to check before you rely on an AI tool for school or work

What you'll build

Syllabus to Study Planner: Use a simple prompt in Google AI Studio to build an AI-powered study planner with a sample syllabus already included.

Your app will:

  • Display a table of quizzes, labs, and exams with their due dates
  • Show the exact sentence from the syllabus for each date
  • Display "⚠️ Check with instructor" when a date is missing instead of guessing
  • Calculate a "Start studying" date for each item
  • Color-code assignments by type

What you'll need

  • A laptop with Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari
  • A personal Google account (Gmail). School or work accounts may have AI Studio turned off.

Don't paste personal information. Use the fictional sample syllabus. Your real syllabus is fine at home, but never paste names, grades, or student IDs.

Prompt 1: Describe your app

Copy this prompt, paste it into the box, and press Enter:

Build a one-page web app called "Syllabus to Study Planner". Keep it small and simple.

- A text box pre-filled with a short FICTIONAL "BIO 101" syllabus with 5 dated items
  (2 quizzes, 1 lab report, 1 midterm, 1 final). Made-up dates, no real names.
- A "Make my plan" button that uses AI once to list each item in a table:
  Item, Type, Due date, and "From your syllabus" (the exact sentence it came from).
- If a date is missing or says TBA, write "⚠️ Check with instructor". Never guess.
- A note at the top: "Don't paste personal info. AI can make mistakes — check every date."

Keep the code minimal: as few files as possible, no login, no database, no extra features.

Note: This first prompt takes a while, about 6 to 8 minutes. AI Studio is writing a whole app: the page, the server, and the files that connect them. You'll see files appear with green checkmarks. Don't refresh, don't close the tab, and don't press stop. It isn't stuck.

While you wait:

  • Watch the file list. Each ✅ is a file the AI wrote for you.
  • Follow along with the instructor's demo of the finished app.
  • Think about it: which of your classes would this help with?

Note: Checkpoint: the right side shows a Preview of your app, with the yellow "Don't paste personal info" banner and the sample BIO 101 syllabus.

AI predicts what sounds right, not what is right. So you'll try to trip it up.

  1. In the syllabus text box, delete the date from one item.
  2. Change another item's date to TBA.
  3. Click Make my plan again.

What should happen: both rows say ⚠️ Check with instructor, with no made-up dates.

Now pick two rows and compare the "From your syllabus" sentence with the syllabus text. Do they match word for word?

If the app invented a date, paste this into the prompt box:

When a due date is missing or says TBA, you invented a date. Never guess dates.
Mark those rows "⚠️ Check with instructor" and leave the date blank.

After every prompt: Once Google AI Studio finishes generating or updating your app, go to the Preview panel and click "Make my plan" to see the updated results. Always test your app after each prompt before moving to the next step.This is the most important step today. Real tools make mistakes like this, and testing is how you catch them before they cost you a missed exam.

What to do

The first build is taking a long time

Normal: 5–7 minutes. Don't refresh or press stop.

A red error in the Preview

Use the rescue prompt and paste the error text into it.

"Make my plan" does nothing

Rescue prompt: "Clicking Make my plan does nothing."

A quota or rate-limit message

You've hit the free limit. Wait a minute and try again, or follow the instructor's demo.

You can't open AI Studio

Use a personal Gmail; school and work accounts may block it.

Next Step - share it

  • Keep working on it: your app is saved in AI Studio. Come back any time and keep prompting.
  • Share it: the Publish button can put your app online with a link. Eligible personal Google accounts can publish up to 2 apps without setting up billing. Google Workspace accounts (many school and work emails) aren't eligible.
  • Download the code: you can download the project as a ZIP. It's a real React app with a small server, not a single HTML page, so hosting it somewhere else takes developer setup. The AI part needs your own Gemini API key on the server.

Remember

  1. Describe it, then improve it: one big prompt to start, small prompts after.
  2. Test before you trust: missing dates, TBA, and checking the quoted sentence.
  3. Protect privacy: fictional or public content only.

Make it your own

  1. Choose one idea and paste its prompt into + New App in Google AI Studio.
  2. Wait for the app to build. Then try adding or completing a task.
  3. Ask AI Studio for one small improvement, such as changing colors or adding a "Due this week" section.
  4. Test your changes. Does the app do what you asked?
